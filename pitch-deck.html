The problem

Miners ship first.
Then they wait — and argue.

Provisional payment is hostage

Junior miners deliver concentrate under offtake contracts, then wait while the buyer holds most of the payment pending final assay.

Grade is a dispute, not a number

Seller lab says 21.50% Co. Buyer lab says 19.10%. Weight, moisture and sampling become leverage to delay or discount payment.

Agents make it riskier

An automated payment agent without hard limits can move too much, too fast — with no CFO cap and no instant freeze when evidence changes.

The result: working-capital squeeze for the seller, counterparty risk for the buyer, and no shared, tamper-evident record either side can act on.

02 / 09
The solution

One certificate. One escrow.
Hard limits on every draw.

  • AssayCertificate (ERC-721) — shipment id, mine, mineral, origin, grade (bps), dry weight and assay URI, minted by a lab attester.
  • AssayEscrow (ERC-20 oUSD) — buyer funds provisional payment; the seller is the only payee.
  • CFO cap + agent allowlist — only allowlisted agents release, and never above the tranche cap.
  • Dispute freeze — CFO or arbiter freezes instantly; every release reverts until cleared.

How it works

From assay to payment — guarded at each step

STEP 1Mint

Lab attester mints the ERC-721 assay certificate: grade, weight, origin and assay URI on-chain.

STEP 2Fund

CFO opens escrow and sets the 20,000 oUSD cap. Buyer funds 100,000 oUSD. Seller is sole payee.

STEP 3Agent release

Agent previews first: RELEASE at 15,000 under cap; REJECT at 25,000 — chain reverts ExceedsTrancheCap.

STEP 4Dispute freeze

Buyer re-assay 19.10% vs 21.50%: arbiter freezes. Agent returns HOLD; releases revert FrozenByDispute.

The agent reads before it sends — and the contract enforces the cap even if the agent is compromised.
